## Merchant dispute rates: floor and shrinkage

A raw dispute ratio is meaningless on a thin denominator. In this dataset **4,600 merchants** have fewer than 3 transactions, and **304 show a raw ratio of 100%** — almost all of them merchants with a single transaction that happened to be disputed.

Two guards are applied:

1. **Denominator floor of 3 transactions.** Below it, `below_floor` is set and the merchant is excluded from rankings. The row is still shown.
2. **Beta-binomial shrinkage toward the category mean**, with the prior strength estimated from the data by method of moments.

### Is there any merchant-level dispute signal to shrink toward?

| eligible merchants (n>=3) | transactions | mean dispute rate | chi-square | df | chi2/df | p-value | overdispersed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3,451 | 12,948 | 12.272% | 3,342.20 | 3,450 | 0.97 | 0.904 | False |

**No.** The spread of merchant dispute rates is no wider than binomial sampling noise would produce on its own — the chi-square statistic sits *below* its degrees of freedom. Every merchant's dispute rate is consistent with one shared underlying probability.

The consequence is deliberate and visible in the data: shrinkage pulls every merchant essentially all the way to its category mean, so `dispute_rate_shrunk_pct` is nearly flat across merchants. **That flatness is the finding, not a bug.** This dataset contains no detectable merchant-level dispute propensity, and we will not manufacture a risk ranking out of sampling noise.

> An earlier run of this same test appeared to find overdispersion (chi2/df = 1.127, p = 1.8e-07). That was an artifact of counting *complaints* rather than *disputed transactions*: 151 transactions carry more than one complaint, which pushes some merchants above a ratio of 1 and breaks the binomial bound the test assumes. Counting distinct disputed transactions removes it.

**What we rank instead:** absolute dispute *exposure* — complaint count and disputed value. A merchant with 5 disputes and a large disputed amount is a genuine operational priority. That is a statement about workload, not about the merchant being riskier than its peers, and the dashboard labels it that way.

## Hour-of-day profile

Resulting profile: min 749, max 841, **coefficient of variation 0.031** — effectively flat. This dataset has no intraday pattern, which is itself worth stating: real UPI traffic does not look like this.

## Hypothesis register

`track1_dataset_notes.txt` ships a list of "insights students may discover". Each is a plausible claim, and each is testable. Asserting them because they sound like fraud analytics is the easiest way to publish something false, so every one is tested and reported whichever way it falls.

Result: **7 NOT SUPPORTED**, **1 BORDERLINE**, **1 CONTRADICTED**, **1 QUANTIFIED**.

| hypothesis | test | statistic | p_value | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Certain merchant categories have disproportionately high chargebacks. | Chi-square homogeneity across 12 categories | chi2=5.241, df=11 | 0.92 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| Some users appear repeatedly in disputes. | Observed vs binomial-expected count of users with >=2 disputes | observed=39, expected=33.9, z=+0.87 | 0.38 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| Some merchants show sudden transaction spikes followed by disputes. | Poisson index of dispersion on daily volume + max merchant-day burst | var/mean=0.901, max merchant-day=3 | 0.74 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| Missing UTRs correlate with failed or disputed transactions. | Chi-square, missing-UTR vs present-UTR, on dispute and on failure | dispute chi2=0.117, failure chi2=0.007 | 0.73 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| Unverified or rejected KYC users show higher dispute risk. | Chi-square homogeneity across 4 KYC statuses, plus per-status tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction | chi2=7.894, df=3, min adjusted pairwise p=0.053 | 0.05 | BORDERLINE |
| Delayed chargeback reporting indicates account takeover or late fraud detection. | Welch t-test on reporting delay, account-takeover vs other reasons (directional) | t=-2.45, mean 5.45 d vs 6.62 d | 0.01 | CONTRADICTED |
| Duplicate transactions inflate revenue and dispute metrics if not removed. | Direct quantification against the raw file | 400 duplicate rows, Rs 5,186,378 | nan | QUANTIFIED |
| Individual merchants differ in how often their transactions are disputed. | Beta-binomial overdispersion test on merchants with >=3 transactions | chi2/df=0.9688, N=3,451 | 0.90 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| Transaction volume follows an intraday pattern. | Chi-square goodness of fit against a uniform 24-hour distribution (date-only timestamps excluded) | chi2=17.5, df=23, CV=0.0310 | 0.79 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| A mismatch between reason code and complaint text is an anomaly signal. | Agreement rate against the independence expectation (one-sample z-test) | agreement=16.83%, expected=16.51%, z=+0.38, n=1,973 | 0.71 | NOT SUPPORTED |

**NOT SUPPORTED — Certain merchant categories have disproportionately high chargebacks.**  
Category rates span 6.00%-13.36% on denominators of about 915 transactions. The spread is what chance alone produces, so ranking categories by dispute rate does not identify a riskier category.

**NOT SUPPORTED — Some users appear repeatedly in disputes.**  
39 customers have two or more disputed transactions; independent disputes at the 12.26% base rate would produce about 34. Repeat disputers exist because customers transact more than once, not because a subgroup is dispute-prone.

**NOT SUPPORTED — Some merchants show sudden transaction spikes followed by disputes.**  
Daily volume is 222 +/- 14 (CV 0.064) across 90 days, and no merchant exceeds 3 transactions in a single day. There is no burst to detect; a spike rule would never fire.

**NOT SUPPORTED — Missing UTRs correlate with failed or disputed transactions.**  
Dispute rate is 12.60% where the UTR is missing against 12.24% where it is present. A missing UTR is a data-quality defect worth fixing in ingestion, not a fraud indicator.

**BORDERLINE — Unverified or rejected KYC users show higher dispute risk.**  
Rates by KYC status: PENDING 11.50%, REJECTED 14.34%, UNKNOWN 11.90%, VERIFIED 13.15%. The omnibus test clears 0.05, but no individual status survives correction for multiple comparisons. The direction also cuts against the claim: VERIFIED customers dispute more often (13.15%) than PENDING ones (11.50%). And KYC resolution was shown to be random with respect to transactions (z = 0.87), so a real effect would be hard to explain. Suggestive at most; not a control we would ship.

**CONTRADICTED — Delayed chargeback reporting indicates account takeover or late fraud detection.**  
The effect is real but points the other way: account-takeover disputes are reported 1.16 days EARLIER than other reasons (5.45 d vs 6.62 d). A rule that escalated late-reported disputes as suspected takeover would systematically look in the wrong place on this data.

**QUANTIFIED — Duplicate transactions inflate revenue and dispute metrics if not removed.**  
Left in place, the 400 byte-identical duplicate transactions would overstate transaction value by Rs 5,186,378 (2.0%) and double-count 84 complaints. This one is straightforwardly true and the pipeline removes them.

**NOT SUPPORTED — Individual merchants differ in how often their transactions are disputed.**  
The spread of merchant dispute rates is no wider than binomial sampling noise. Every merchant is consistent with one shared 12.27% probability, so merchant dispute rate carries no information and must not drive a risk score.

**NOT SUPPORTED — Transaction volume follows an intraday pattern.**  
Excluding the 1,000 date-only timestamps that parse to midnight, hourly volume is flat (CV 0.031). The apparent midnight peak in the raw data is a parsing artifact, not customer behaviour — worth knowing before anyone builds an off-hours rule.

**NOT SUPPORTED — A mismatch between reason code and complaint text is an anomaly signal.**  
Where the complaint text names a specific cause, the structured reason code agrees with it 16.83% of the time, against 16.51% expected if the two fields were unrelated. They carry no mutual information, so a mismatch means nothing and is reported as a separate dimension rather than scored.
